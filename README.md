## Overview

AI infrastructure development is increasing attention on the electricity required to operate data centers. The timing and location of deployment may depend partly on access to reliable power, alongside compute availability, construction, permitting, networking, water, and other inputs.

This repository develops an **evidence-weighted research framework** for evaluating how power availability may influence long-term capital allocation across the AI infrastructure value chain.

Rather than beginning with individual companies or investment ideas, the research follows a structured sequence:

1. Validate whether power availability is becoming a binding constraint.
2. Identify where the constraint exists.
3. Determine who owns or controls the constraint.
4. Analyze which infrastructure segments are exposed.
5. Develop a capital allocation framework.
6. Map representative public companies.
7. Build a long-term monitoring framework.

The objective is **not** to provide stock recommendations. Instead, the project aims to build a transparent and repeatable research process that connects infrastructure evidence with investment implications.

## Research Question

> **How should investors evaluate the AI infrastructure value chain if power availability becomes a material, region-specific deployment constraint?**

## Research Modules

### Part I: Thesis Validation

#### Module 1: Thesis Validation

**Question:** Is power availability becoming a binding constraint for AI infrastructure growth?

**Objective:** Evaluate the available evidence supporting this hypothesis. If the evidence is sufficiently strong, subsequent modules examine where the constraint exists, who controls it, and how it may influence long-term capital allocation.

---

### Part II: Constraint Mapping

#### Module 2: Regional Constraint Mapping

**Question:** Where are the most significant power constraints?

**Objective:** Identify the regions, grid operators, and electricity markets where power availability is most likely to limit AI infrastructure deployment.

#### Module 3: Constraint Ownership

**Question:** Who owns or controls the constraint?

**Objective:** Determine whether the constraint is primarily associated with regulated utilities, generation owners, transmission operators, interconnection rights, regulators, equipment suppliers, or large electricity consumers.

---

### Part III: Capital Allocation

#### Module 4: Value Chain Analysis

**Question:** Which parts of the AI power value chain are exposed, and under what conditions could that exposure affect economics?

**Objective:** Connect the identified constraints to infrastructure segments including electricity generation, transmission, distribution, grid equipment, utilities, and power procurement.

#### Module 5: Capital Allocation Framework

**Question:** How may capital allocation change under different power constraint scenarios?

**Objective:** Translate the research findings into a structured capital allocation framework across the AI infrastructure ecosystem.

#### Module 6: Public Equity Mapping

**Question:** Which public companies are exposed to the identified infrastructure segments?

**Objective:** Map representative public companies to each infrastructure segment while distinguishing between direct, indirect, and conditional exposure.

#### Module 7: Watchlist and Monitoring

**Question:** Which indicators should be monitored as the thesis evolves?

**Objective:** Maintain a structured watchlist of regions, companies, infrastructure indicators, regulatory developments, and primary data sources that may strengthen or weaken the investment thesis over time.

## Methodology

The research framework follows seven core principles:

- Evidence-weighted analysis
- Primary-source validation
- Regional constraint mapping
- Infrastructure ownership analysis
- Value chain exposure analysis
- Capital allocation logic
- Continuous monitoring and thesis revision

Where possible, conclusions are supported by publicly available primary sources, including regional grid operators, utilities, government agencies, regulatory filings, company disclosures, and independent infrastructure research.

---

## Current Focus

The initial thesis-validation review and three-region comparison are complete. They support continued research but do not establish company recommendations, relative returns, or a definitive bottleneck ranking.

Current research is transitioning from identifying **where power constraints exist** to determining **who owns or controls the infrastructure affected by those constraints**.

The next module examines utilities, transmission owners, generation companies, interconnection rights, regulators, and other participants that influence electricity availability for AI infrastructure.
